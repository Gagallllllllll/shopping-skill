# Category pack: sport

Sports gear for any sport: snowboard, ski, running, cycling, climbing, surf, racket sports, golf, fitness. Status: **tested** (first real session 2026-11-16, snowboard).

## Interview add-ons

**Level and practice**
- Level: beginner, intermediate, advanced, expert. How many years? Lessons or coaching?
- Frequency: days or sessions per year, and how long each one lasts.
- Goals for the next 1 to 2 seasons: progress, comfort, performance, competition, fun.
- Style or discipline as a split: "out of 10 sessions, how many on X, Y, Z?"

**Current gear as the reference**
- Current model of each item, its flex or stiffness if known, and how it feels: too soft, right, too stiff, and where (for boots: ankle folding on landings, lack of power when carving, foot pain). Set the target **relative to the current gear** (for example "flex 5 feels too soft" → target about 6.5 to 7.5), not only from the level and style.

**Body and fit**
- Height, weight, dominant side or stance if relevant.
- Shoe size and foot shape (wide, narrow, high instep); inseam and arm length for bikes; hand size for rackets and gloves.
- Past injuries or sensitive joints (general information only).

**Conditions**
- Where: countries, resorts, terrain, indoor or outdoor, altitude.
- Typical conditions: hard snow or powder, mud or road, cold or heat, wet or dry.
- Transport and storage.

**Per sport**
- **Snowboard:** boot size and brand (compare with the current board's width: a wide board with medium boots feels slow edge to edge), stance (regular or goofy), current bindings, binding entry wanted (classic straps, quick entry with normal boots such as FASE or rear entry, or a step-in system that needs its own boots), preferred feel (playful or stable), carving or butters, speed.
- **Ski:** current ski length, turn style (short or long), boot flex and last width, touring or resort only.
- **Running:** weekly distance, race goals, pace, terrain (road, trail, track), pronation if known, current shoe and what you like about it.
- **Cycling:** road, gravel, MTB, commuting; distances and climbing; position (racy or comfortable); e-bike ok?
- **Climbing:** indoor or outdoor, bouldering or routes, foot shape, comfort or performance.
- **Surf:** wave type and size, current board volume, paddling fitness, session frequency.
- **Racket sports and golf:** playing style, injuries (tennis elbow), swing speed or handicap.
- **Fitness:** space at home, goals (strength, cardio), noise limits, budget per piece.

## Turning needs into target specs

- Size from the brand's own chart, and choose the size where the client's weight or measurement sits **in the middle** of the range, not at the edge.
- When charts, users and testers disagree, prefer a tester with a build close to the client's, then the brand chart, and show the conflict.
- **Snowboard width vs boot:** whenever boots are chosen or change, ask the boot's outsole length in cm and compare it with the board width at the inserts (waist + stance²/sidecut radius, about +1 cm on a 156). Overhang per side ≈ (outsole × cos(angle) − width at inserts) / 2; up to about 2 cm per side is usually fine (recommendation). Reduced-footprint boots can fit one size narrower than their label.
- Compatibility across a set: board inserts and binding discs, binding size and boot size, bike standards (wheel, axle, brake mount), shoe last and binding or pedal system.

## Trusted sources

- Independent tests with named testers and scores (for snowboard, for example The Good Ride and Whitelines; look for the equivalent for each sport), long-term reviews.
- Rider and athlete forums (English, French, German), video reviews and their comments. Reddit often blocks automated reading.
- Brand spec pages and size charts for the current season.

## Price and timing

- New models arrive before the season; last season's models drop 20 to 40% and sell out fast in common sizes.
- End-of-season sales, Black Friday, test and ex-rental fleets sold by shops at season end.

## Second-hand checks

- **Boards and skis:** base (deep scratches, repairs), edges (rust, cracks, dents), sidewalls, delamination, core damage near tips and tails, model year (sticker or serial).
- **Bindings:** cracks on baseplate and highback, straps and ladders, ratchets, discs and screws included; quick-entry systems: confirm the exact version; Burton EST fits Channel boards only, Re:Flex fits 4x2/4x4; missing discs or screws lower the fair price by the replacement cost.
- **Boots:** label size (US and Mondo), liner pack-out and heat-moulding, sole glue, BOA dials and lace; boots are best bought new unless the fit is certain.
- **Bikes:** frame cracks, drivetrain wear, proof of ownership.
- Pay only inside the platform; ask for the model year and close-up photos before any offer.

## Safety

- Helmet and protection advice where relevant (marked recommendation).
- Avalanche safety gear for off-piste: general information and training advice only.
- Injuries: general information only, refer to a professional (physio, doctor, boot fitter).
